Chiefs Rout Denver, Giants Stun Dallas and Chicago Sets a Scoring Record in Week 1

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The NFL's opening week produced the highest-scoring season opener the league has ever recorded, a rout on Monday night in Kansas City, and a debut win in New York that nobody in Dallas saw coming. With Week 2 opening Thursday, here is where the season actually stands.

Start with the game that broke the record. Chicago beat Carolina 59-37, and the combined 96 points make it the highest-scoring opener in NFL history. The Bears led 14-7 after the first quarter, 31-24 at half, and never trailed. Caleb Williams threw for 269 yards and two touchdowns, and D'Andre Swift carried 18 times for 124 yards and three scores.

Kansas City closed the week on Monday night by routing Denver 31-10. The Broncos scored a touchdown in the opening quarter and then nothing but a field goal for the rest of the night. Patrick Mahomes and the Chiefs pulled away in the second half and never let the game get close again.

The result Dallas will be thinking about all week came a night earlier. The Giants beat the Cowboys 28-20 to give John Harbaugh a win in his first game as New York's head coach. The Giants scored in every quarter. Dallas was shut out in the first and never led.

Detroit's win over New Orleans took the longest to settle. The Lions won 31-30 in overtime after Saints coach Kellen Moore chose to go for two and the win rather than kick the extra point and play on. It did not work. Detroit escaped, New Orleans went home 0-1, and the decision will follow Moore for a while.

Baltimore made the loudest statement. The Ravens beat Indianapolis 41-23 behind three rushing touchdowns from Derrick Henry, who at this stage of his career still looks like the most physically punishing runner in the league. Lamar Jackson had the kind of night that starts MVP conversations in September.

Around the rest of the slate: Minnesota came back to beat Green Bay 39-22, scoring 22 in the fourth quarter alone. Jacksonville routed Cleveland 34-10 as Trevor Lawrence threw four touchdown passes. Buffalo edged Houston 36-31 after Josh Allen found Joshua Palmer late. Philadelphia beat Washington 24-22 and stuffed a two-point try at the end. Arizona won 26-14 at the Chargers in Mike LaFleur's head coaching debut. Las Vegas beat Miami 27-13 behind Kirk Cousins, who reached 301 career touchdown passes. Pittsburgh got past Atlanta 20-13 on a T.J. Watt pick-six. Cincinnati forced four turnovers in a 33-27 win over Tampa Bay. The Jets beat Tennessee 23-10, and San Francisco beat the Rams 27-7 in the league's first game played in Australia.

Nine teams are 1-0, nine are 0-1, and the rest opened with ties or have yet to settle into either group. The polls and power rankings released this week are working off exactly one data point each, which is why the next few Sundays matter more than this one did.

Week 2 opens Thursday night with Detroit at Buffalo, two 1-0 teams meeting in Orchard Park. The Sunday slate that follows has real questions attached to it.

The biggest belongs to Seattle. The defending Super Bowl champions ruled Sam Darnold out against Arizona with a glute injury that knocked him out of the opener on the fifth play, when his knee hit the turf on a sack from behind. Drew Lock starts instead. Seattle is not putting Darnold on injured reserve, which would cost him four games, so the team clearly expects him back sooner, though sources have told ESPN to brace for roughly a month.

Cincinnati has a quieter version of the same problem. Joe Burrow has back tightness that surfaced in the hours after the Tampa Bay win, and he made no significant throws in Wednesday's open practice, taking what he called "mental reps." He was blunt about whether sitting out was under consideration. "That's ridiculous," he said. "If I'm not having surgery, I'll be out there."

Minnesota lost a piece of its backfield for longer. The Vikings placed running back Jordan Mason on injured reserve after thumb surgery, which moves Aaron Jones up the depth chart before a trip to Chicago to face a Bears team that just scored 59 points.

What Week 1 established is that very little is established. A record-setting shootout, a rout on Monday night, an overtime finish decided by a two-point call, and a coaching debut that went badly for Dallas. By Sunday night there will be twice as much to go on.
