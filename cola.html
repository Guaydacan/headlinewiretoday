2027 Social Security COLA Projected at 3.6%, Largest Boost Since 2023

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The Social Security Administration is expected to announce a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 on October 14, the largest annual increase since 2023. The adjustment would add roughly $74 a month to the average benefit, a meaningful change for the roughly 71 million Americans who receive Social Security.

The figure is not final. The COLA is calculated from the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, known as the CPI-W, and the last piece of that calculation, September inflation data, is not released until mid-October. Current projections from the Senior Citizens League put the increase at 3.6%, while other analyses estimate as high as 3.8%.

How the number is set

Since 1983, the annual adjustment has been based on the third-quarter CPI-W. The Social Security Administration compares the average for July, August and September of the current year against the same three months a year earlier, and the percentage difference becomes the COLA that takes effect the following January.

That is why the announcement lands on October 14: it is timed to the Bureau of Labor Statistics release of September CPI-W data, the final input the formula needs. Beneficiaries are notified of the exact amount and its effect on their monthly payment shortly after.

The 3.6% projection is a step up from the 2.8% adjustment for 2026, but it has been drifting downward as inflation cools. The Senior Citizens League revised its estimate from 3.8% to 3.6% in August, and further cooling could push the final number lower still.

For many retirees, the concern is not the headline percentage but whether it keeps pace with the costs that actually matter. Housing, medical care and groceries have all risen faster than the overall inflation measure used to set the COLA, and advocacy groups have argued for years that the CPI-W understates what older households spend.

If the 3.6% figure holds, the average retired worker would see an increase of about $74 a month, with the exact amount depending on the individual benefit. The change takes effect with the January 2027 payment.

Until the official announcement, the number remains an estimate. The final COLA could land above or below the current projection depending on September's inflation data, and the Social Security Administration will confirm the figure and the resulting payment amounts in mid-October.

For anyone planning a budget around next year's benefits, the practical step is to treat 3.6% as a working estimate rather than a guarantee, and to watch for the October 14 announcement.
