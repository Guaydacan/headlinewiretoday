An American Man Will Play for the US Open Title for the First Time Since 2024

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

Two American men won marathon five-set quarterfinals within hours of each other at Flushing Meadows, and the result is something American tennis has not had in two years: a guaranteed home player in the US Open final.

Ben Shelton, the No. 8 seed, and Frances Tiafoe, seeded 11th, will meet in Friday's semifinal at Arthur Ashe Stadium. Whoever wins plays for the title on Sunday. The last American man to reach a US Open final was Taylor Fritz in 2024.

Shelton ends the defending champion's run

Shelton's quarterfinal against Carlos Alcaraz went the distance and finished as the latest match in US Open history. Alcaraz, the defending champion and No. 2 seed, had been the favorite for most of the fortnight after world No. 1 Jannik Sinner withdrew before the tournament with a knee injury.

His exit guaranteed two things at once: a first-time US Open men's champion, and a first-time major finalist out of the bottom half of the draw.

Tiafoe's route was no gentler. He came through Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (1), 6-4, 7-6 (6) in the fourth round, then won another long one to reach the last four.

A draw that has punished its own seeds

The men's tournament has been unusually brutal at the top. Six of the top eight seeds are already out. Novak Djokovic went first, beaten by Mariano Navone in five sets on day one, ending a run of 78 consecutive first-round wins at majors and marking his earliest major exit in 20 years.

Felix Auger-Aliassime, the No. 3 seed, fell to Karen Khachanov in the second round. Alex de Minaur, seeded 6th, lost to Botic van de Zandschulp in the same round. Flavio Cobolli went out in the third. Taylor Fritz blew a two-set lead against Francisco Cerundolo and lost in five.

Alexander Zverev, the No. 1 seed, is the last of the original top three standing. He beat van de Zandschulp in straight sets to reach the semifinals, where Khachanov awaits after Alexander Blockx retired from their quarterfinal.

The women's draw reaches its last four

Aryna Sabalenka arrived as the two-time defending champion and top seed. A third straight title would make her the first woman to win three in a row in New York since Serena Williams in 2012, 2013 and 2014.

Coco Gauff, the 2023 champion, came through a three-set quarterfinal against Mirra Andreeva to stay in the draw. Elena Rybakina beat Qinwen Zheng, ending a run that had begun in qualifying and taken out Madison Keys along the way.

The women's semifinals are Thursday and the final is Saturday, a day before the men close the tournament and the 2026 Grand Slam season.
