Nearly 30,000 Pounds of Imported Beef Recalled After Inspection Step Was Skipped

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

Federal regulators have recalled nearly 30,000 pounds of raw beef imported from Argentina after the shipment entered the United States without undergoing a mandatory inspection step. The U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Food Safety and Inspection Service announced the action on August 7, covering 29,628 pounds of product distributed to retailers in Florida and Texas.

The importer, Corte Argentino USA LLC of Aventura, Florida, brought the meat into the country without presenting it for import reinspection. That checkpoint is not optional. Every shipment of foreign meat, poultry and egg product must be examined by FSIS after clearing U.S. Customs and Border Protection, and inspectors review documentation, labeling, packaging and the physical condition of the product before it is allowed into commerce.

What was recalled and how to identify it

The affected beef was produced between May 15 and May 20 of this year and carries use-or-freeze-by dates between September 15 and September 20. It was sold under the Frigorifico Gorina SAIC brand in cardboard boxes of varying weights. The cuts include boneless top sirloin butt, eye round, topside cap off, flat and knuckle.

Products bear the Argentinian establishment number “EST. N° OF. 2025” and the shipping mark “26644-AA.” Those markings are the reliable way to identify the recalled meat, since the boxes themselves vary in size and weight.

FSIS has classified this as a Class I recall, the agency’s most serious category. A Class I designation means there is a reasonable probability that consuming the product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death. That classification reflects the regulatory breach rather than any confirmed contamination, and no illnesses or injuries have been reported. Because the product skipped the checkpoint entirely, regulators have no verified information about its condition when it entered the country, so the highest risk category applies by default.

What consumers should do now

Regulators say their main concern is that the beef may already be sitting in home refrigerators and freezers. The freeze-by dates extend into mid-September, so the window in which this meat remains in circulation has not closed.

Consumers who purchased the product should not eat it. FSIS advises throwing it away or returning it to the place of purchase. Anyone with food safety questions can reach the USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline at 888-674-6854, or file a report through the agency’s Electronic Consumer Complaint Monitoring System.

The USDA has not published a list of specific stores. The recall notice states only that the products went to distributors and retailers in Florida and Texas, and it remains unclear whether those distributors moved any of the meat beyond those two states.

Not an isolated case

This is the second recall of its kind in recent months tied to the same type of import paperwork failure. Earlier this summer, FSIS issued another Class I recall covering more than 12,000 pounds of smoked bacon imported from Canada.

Argentine beef accounts for a small share of American beef imports, roughly two percent by most estimates. The recall arrives during a broader debate over expanding those imports, and it has drawn attention from cattle industry groups who have questioned whether increased foreign supply can be adequately inspected.

For shoppers, the practical takeaway is narrower. Check the freezer, look for the establishment number and shipping mark, and do not eat anything that matches.
