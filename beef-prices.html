DOJ Expands Beef Price Investigation to Kroger, Walmart, Costco and Five More Retailers

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The Department of Justice's Antitrust Division has sent letters to eight of the country's largest grocery retailers, requesting detailed information about their beef pricing as part of an expanding investigation into record-high beef prices. Kroger, Walmart, Publix, Albertsons, Aldi, Ahold Delhaize, Costco and Amazon all received the letters, dated July 14, from Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward.

The move widens a probe that began in May 2026 targeting the four largest meatpackers, who together control roughly 85% of the U.S. fed cattle market. Investigators are now asking retailers directly for data on beef pricing strategies, profit margins, costs and purchasing arrangements dating back to 2020.

Why beef got this expensive

Beef and veal prices rose 9.4% in the twelve months ending in July, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, far outpacing the broader 3.4% inflation rate. Ground beef alone is up roughly 24% since early 2025, now averaging $6.89 a pound. The U.S. cattle herd has fallen to its lowest level in 75 years, a result of years of drought, high input costs and herd liquidation that economists say is the primary driver of the price spike.

The DOJ's letters ask each retailer to brief investigators on how their beef pricing and margins have changed since 2020, what has driven their own cost increases, and how they negotiate wholesale purchasing arrangements with meatpackers.

Independent producer group R-CALF USA said it supports the expanded investigation, pointing to a long-standing gap between what ranchers are paid for cattle and what consumers pay at the register. "That gap raises serious questions about whether market power is contributing disproportionately to consumer beef prices," the group said in a statement.

Not everyone agrees the answer is antitrust enforcement. Economists point to the historically low cattle inventory, prolonged drought conditions and steady consumer demand as the clearer explanation, arguing the supply shortage would push prices up regardless of retailer pricing behavior.

Beef prices are expected to stay elevated well into 2027, according to Sylvain Charlebois, a food distribution and policy professor at Dalhousie University, who told Newsweek the cattle shortage will take years to resolve. In the meantime, the administration has taken other steps aimed at consumers directly, including a plan to allow 300,000 tons of imported ground beef into the country at reduced tariffs, in exchange for a 25% lower retail price on those products.

It remains unclear how quickly that imported beef will reach shelves, or whether the DOJ's retailer investigation will lead to any enforcement action. For now, shoppers can expect beef prices to stay a source of sticker shock at checkout.

The Antitrust Division has not set a public deadline for retailers to respond to the July letters.
