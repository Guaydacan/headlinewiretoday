Where the Week's Biggest Questions Get Answered Saturday

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

College football's attention this week has narrowed onto a handful of programs, and the story at the top is drawing more than double anything else. It is not a team. It is a coach. Lane Kiffin left Ole Miss for LSU after last season, went back to Oxford last Saturday as the visiting coach, was booed from early in the day, and lost 32-24. Afterward: "Sometimes you can't predict your emotions. I know sometimes I don't act like it but I'm a human, too." He also took credit for the early crowd: "you're welcome."

That afternoon splits into two games. First, Texas A&M, No. 23 and 2-1, at No. 10 LSU, also 2-1, at 7:30 p.m. ET in Tiger Stadium on ABC, LSU by nine and a half. Both arrive off their first loss, in the first meeting between Kiffin and A&M's Mike Elko as head coaches. LSU quarterback Sam Leavitt is off the availability report, so he plays, after a week of questions about bailing out of the pocket in Oxford. Elko, whose defense gave up 31 at home, says it must "catch up quick."

Second, with the strangest number on the board: No. 4 Ole Miss, 3-0, visits No. 21 Florida, also 3-0, at 3:30 p.m. ET in Gainesville on ABC. Florida is favored by three and a half at home despite ranking 17 places lower, and quarterback Trinidad Chambliss has to answer that.

No. 8 Alabama, 3-0, hosts South Carolina at 6 p.m. ET at Bryant-Denny on ESPN by twelve and a half, built on last week's 50-36 win over Florida State. Keelon Russell has three starts and just won Shaun Alexander player of the week, and Kalen DeBoer says he improves "every game." South Carolina has the opposite question: LaNorris Sellers has had two rocky outings and Shane Beamer will not bench him.

Tennessee opens the day at noon ET against No. 1 Texas at Neyland Stadium on ABC, Texas by four, both 3-0. These programs have never met on either campus and have not played since the Cotton Bowl on New Year's Day 1969, so the country's top-ranked team walks into Neyland cold.

North Carolina is the exception. The Tar Heels are still being asked about after last Saturday's 28-20 collapse at Clemson, and they do not play: the bye gives Bill Belichick a week to fix it.

Tune-ups in Texas, a test in the ACC, four teams answering last week

The pairing behind Texas Tech's 28-26 win over Houston now goes two ways. No. 11 Texas Tech, 3-0, hosts Sam Houston at noon ET on TNT by thirty-four and a half, a tune-up; Tech and Texas are the state's last two unbeatens. No. 25 Houston, 2-1, is at Georgia Southern at 4 p.m. ET on ESPNU by seventeen and a half, where Willie Fritz went 17-7 in 2014 and 2015; he calls it a business trip, not a homecoming. Miami, No. 6 and 3-0, hosts Central Michigan at 6:30 p.m. ET by forty-one and a half, the local argument being whether Cristobal rests starters.

Wake Forest at No. 16 Louisville, noon ET on ESPN by thirteen and a half, is the conference's real test; Jake Dickert says there has been "too much of the Deacs beating the Deacs." Vanderbilt, 3-0, is at Auburn at 4:15 p.m. ET on SEC Network by nine and a half, after beating NC State 35-31 on an end-zone fumble with a second left. Auburn has not won an SEC home game since 2024, and Hugh Freeze takes the rumors about his job "in stride."

Colorado, 2-1, is at Baylor at noon ET on ESPN2 by nine and a half after a 41-7 beating at Northwestern, the heaviest Power Four loss of the weekend. Freshman Julian Lewis threw two interceptions, and Deion Sanders has told fans to judge the response at Baylor. West Virginia, 3-0, hosts Oklahoma State at 7 p.m. ET on FS1 by a point and a half in the Big 12 opener for both. UTEP hosts Oregon State at 9 p.m. ET in the Sun Bowl for homecoming, both 1-2, by ten and a half, off national television.

Who is out, who is back, and who is a game-time call

Texas A&M is without receiver Terry Bussey, carted off after the opening kickoff against Kentucky and facing a long recovery; Jamarion Morrow and Ashton Bethel-Roman are questionable. LSU's concern is its secondary. Ole Miss has six ruled out, and Kewan Lacy is a questionable game-time call after an encouraging MRI. Alabama has ruled out edge rushers Justin Hill and Desmond Umeozulu, a South Carolina transfer, plus corner Jorden Edmonds and defensive back Nick Sherman, and lost linebacker Cayden Jones and lineman Jeremiah Beaman for the season; Noah Rogers and AK Dear are back. South Carolina is without Gabriel Brownlow-Dindy, with Dylan Stewart probable.

Tennessee has ruled out five, including safety Dylan Lewis and tight end Ethan Davis, with running back Javin Gordon and defensive lineman Nathan Robinson questionable, while leading rusher DeSean Bishop is off the report entirely after being shaken up last week, which points to him playing. Baylor gets quarterback DJ Lagway back from an ankle injury.

If your team is on this list, Saturday settles the argument you have had all week. Check the availability report on game morning, because the calls above are genuinely open, then go find your kickoff.
