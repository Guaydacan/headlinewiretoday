American Hopes Collapse on a Brutal Day at the US Open

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The third round of the US Open turned into one of the most punishing days of the tournament for the American contingent, with three former Grand Slam finalists knocked out inside a few hours at Flushing Meadows on Saturday.

The most dramatic exit belonged to Madison Keys. The No. 22 seed led Zheng Qinwen 6-1, and then 5-0 in the deciding set. She served for the match at 5-1 and held a match point. She did not win another game, losing 1-6, 7-6(3), 7-5.

A comeback from nowhere

Zheng, the Olympic gold medalist from the 2024 Paris Games, entered this tournament ranked No. 121 and had to win three qualifying matches just to reach the main draw. She is still working her way back from elbow surgery that pushed her down from a career-high ranking of No. 4.

"I'm just telling myself keep holding every single point, never give up, stay there and somehow little by little it just happened," Zheng said afterward. "The hard work pays off."

Keys, who won the 2025 Australian Open for her only major title, was candid about how quickly it turned. "I feel like I obviously got off to a really good lead, and then I just got really, I mean, got really scared, like I couldn't swing," she said. "Tennis just feels really hard right now, so I feel like that kind of took over."

At one point during the collapse she banged her racket against her head repeatedly and pulled off her visor in frustration. Two days earlier, in the previous round, Keys had been the one mounting the comeback, saving five match points to win a third-set tiebreaker.

Fritz and Anisimova follow her out

The men's draw lost its highest-profile American the same afternoon. No. 9 seed Taylor Fritz built a two-set lead over No. 24 Francisco Cerundolo and then lost three straight, going down 3-6, 4-6, 6-3, 6-4, 6-4. Fritz had come through his first two rounds in straight sets without any hint of trouble.

Cerundolo, playing on Arthur Ashe Stadium for the first time, said the surroundings stopped bothering him as the match wore on. "I think fourth and fifth I played a really good tennis that I wasn't finding that tennis in the previous sets and the previous rounds," he said.

Amanda Anisimova, a US Open runner-up last year, went out to No. 24 Anastasia Potapova 6-2, 7-5. Anisimova converted just 3 of her 16 break point chances, a 19 percent rate that decided a match she spent most of chasing.

What is left in the draw

Iga Swiatek came through her own test, beating Marie Bouzkova in two tiebreaks to set up a meeting with Zheng in the next round. Coco Gauff, the 2023 champion here, was scheduled against Cristina Bucsa with the winner advancing to face the survivor of Alexandra Eala and Iva Jovic.

The tournament runs through the finals on September 13. With three former finalists gone in a single afternoon, the second week opens with a draw that looks considerably different than it did on Saturday morning.
