Burrow Says Sitting Out Would Be 'Ridiculous' as Week 2 Injury Reports Reshape Sunday

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The Wednesday injury report is usually a formality. This week it is the story. With Sunday's Week 2 slate three days out, the biggest questions in the NFL are not about matchups or schemes. They are about which quarterbacks are going to be standing on the field when their teams kick off.

Start in Cincinnati, where Joe Burrow spent Wednesday's practice taking what he called "mental reps." Burrow has back tightness that showed up in the hours after the Bengals' 33-27 win over Tampa Bay, and he made no significant throws during the portion of practice open to reporters. Asked whether sitting out against Houston as a precaution was on the table, he did not hedge.

"That's ridiculous," Burrow said.

He was sacked only once against the Buccaneers. The hit he thinks did it came on a scramble for a first down, a play that extended a drive that ended in a touchdown. He said he would make the same decision again. "I was comfortable with it. It was either that or kick a field goal."

The context behind that answer matters. Burrow missed nine games last season with turf toe, the third time in seven years he has landed on injured reserve. Cincinnati has missed the playoffs three years running after reaching a Super Bowl at the start of the decade, and Burrow spent the offseason talking about urgency. His standard for Sunday was blunt: "If I'm not having surgery, I'll be out there."

In Seattle the answer went the other way. The Seahawks ruled Sam Darnold out against Arizona on Wednesday with a glute injury that knocked him out of the opener on the fifth play, when his knee hit the turf as he was sacked from behind. The team called it a "very unique soft-tissue" injury. Drew Lock starts instead, which means the reigning Super Bowl champions go into a divisional road game with their backup at quarterback.

Seattle is not putting Darnold on injured reserve, and that decision says something. An IR stint would cost him at least four games. Keeping him on the 53-man roster signals the Seahawks think he can beat that timeline, though sources have told ESPN to brace for roughly four weeks, with a Week 5 return against San Francisco floated as realistic.

Atlanta is the genuine unknown. Michael Penix Jr. has not played since tearing his ACL in November and was only cleared for full practice on August 24. On Wednesday he split first-team reps with Cooper Rush, who started the Week 1 loss to Pittsburgh and is now dealing with back spasms of his own. Tua Tagovailoa, the third name in that room, sat out Wednesday with an oblique injury.

Penix would not commit either way. "I'm close," he said. "It's just a couple technical things that I want to hit, some goals that I have that I want to achieve before I do step foot out on that field." Head coach Kevin Stefanski's position on the quarterback job was that he is "not ruling anybody out."

New Orleans got the week's clearest good news. Alvin Kamara practiced in full on Wednesday after spraining his MCL in an August joint practice with Dallas, an injury that was supposed to cost him a month. Coach Kellen Moore said Kamara could have played in Week 1 and is now "in a great place" for Sunday's trip to Baltimore, where the Saints will need him: they managed 91 rushing yards as a team against Detroit.

Minnesota's news went the other direction and is already reshaping backfields. The Vikings placed running back Jordan Mason on injured reserve after thumb surgery, which moves Aaron Jones up the depth chart ahead of a Week 2 trip to Chicago. Indianapolis has a separate problem: receiver Keenan Allen was formally charged with operating while intoxicated, facing two misdemeanors, with a reported .224 reading.

The schedule underneath all of this is the reason the reports matter. Week 2 opens Thursday night with Detroit at Buffalo, a rematch of two 1-0 teams with the Bills favored by four and a half and a total of 54.5, the highest on the board. Sunday brings Cowboys and Commanders at a 50.5 total, Vikings at Bears between two teams that both won in Week 1, Eagles at Titans, and Seahawks at Cardinals with Lock under center. Kansas City hosts Indianapolis on Sunday night after routing Denver 31-10.

Nine teams sit at 1-0 and nine at 0-1 after a Week 1 that included the highest-scoring opener in NFL history, Chicago's 59-37 win over Carolina. By Sunday night that split gets resolved into something closer to a real standings picture. Whether it does so with Burrow, Penix and Kamara on the field is the question the next three days will answer.
