Three Top-Ten Teams Went Down in One Saturday and the Rankings Are a Mess

By Headline Wire Today Staff Published April 6, 2026 6 min read

The first three weekends of a college football season are supposed to be a warm-up. Nobody told the teams that. On Saturday, September 12, the sport's top-ranked team lost at home, a top-ten team lost outright as a heavy favorite, and a third top-fifteen team went down on the road, all inside about eight hours.

The headline result came out of Austin. Ohio State, ranked No. 1 and playing like it for three quarters, led Texas 23-3 going into the fourth. The Longhorns then scored 21 unanswered points and won 24-23. It was a twenty-point comeback against the best team in the country, and it came in the fourth quarter of a game that had already felt decided.

That result alone would have reshuffled the top of the polls. It was not the only one.

In Stillwater, Oklahoma State beat No. 6 Oregon 39-31, a result almost nobody had on their card. Oregon had opened the season ranked in the top ten and was widely treated as a playoff lock. Oklahoma State had lost the week before. The Cowboys scored 21 points in the second quarter and never gave the lead back.

In Ann Arbor, Michigan beat No. 11 Oklahoma 17-10 on a one-yard touchdown run set up by an interception in the middle of the fourth quarter. It was a low-scoring, defensive game of the kind that used to define the sport before the offenses took over, and it moved Michigan to 2-0 while dropping Oklahoma to 1-1.

What makes this stretch unusual is not that upsets happened. Upsets happen every September. It is that they happened to three teams that were all supposed to be in the playoff conversation in December, and they happened on the same afternoon, which means the rankings released this week are essentially a rewrite rather than an adjustment.

Underneath those results, the week produced the kind of individual performances that carry a season. Ohio State's Jeremiah Smith is at 16 catches for 315 yards and three touchdowns through two games. Iowa's Kamari Moulton has 240 rushing yards. Colorado State's Hunter Hejny has thrown nine touchdown passes. USC's Jayden Maiava sits at 896 passing yards and 10 touchdowns, the best raw numbers of anyone in the country so far.

The teams that survived the weekend cleanly now inherit the spotlight. Georgia beat Western Kentucky 70-20 and travels to Arkansas. Notre Dame shut out Rice 52-0 and hosts Michigan State. Indiana handled Howard 55-0. Miami put 77 points on Florida A&M. Those are not tests, and the polls know it, which is why the next real measuring stick matters so much.

That measuring stick arrives Saturday, September 19. LSU, ranked No. 7 and 2-0, travels to No. 8 Ole Miss in Oxford, a three-point game on paper and the only matchup on the slate between two teams currently inside the top ten. Michigan State goes to Notre Dame. SMU visits Louisville in a game the books have at a point and a half. West Virginia meets Virginia in Charlotte.

The rest of the weekend is the usual September mix of tune-ups: Kent State at Ohio State, Buffalo at Penn State, UTEP at Michigan, Western Kentucky at Indiana. Those games will not change anything at the top. The ones in Oxford, South Bend and Louisville might.

What last Saturday established is that this season's hierarchy is not settled and probably will not be for another month. Three of the teams that were supposed to be at the top of it lost inside a single afternoon. The teams that replace them in the rankings this week have played a combined schedule that has told us very little. The next few Saturdays will tell us more.
